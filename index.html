Open Tools, Open Sky: Doing LSST Science with Public Rubin Data & LINCC Frameworks

About

Rubin Observatory's alert stream is the largest public astronomical data product ever released. Every alert packet, and every broker classification, is available to every astronomer — regardless of data-rights status. Yet without structured onboarding and the right tool guidance, that access remains opaque for many Latin American researchers.

Over three days in Medellín, students, early-career researchers, and senior LATAM scientists — working in teams paired with Rubin mentors — will build functioning science pipelines on broker-served Rubin alerts using LINCC Frameworks tools. The event follows the sprint-driven model of the Rubin Alerts & AI Hackathon: short context talks, pre-formed teams, daily stand-ups, and hack sprints punctuated by rapid team updates and a final show-and-tell.

Goal 01

Onboard a new LATAM cohort

Bring researchers to Rubin's public products (alert packets, brokers) and to LINCC Frameworks — LSDB/HATS, Hyrax, LightCurveLynx — with particular attention to those without individual LSST data rights.

Goal 02

Pair every team with a mentor

Each team is paired with a Rubin mentor committed to structured post-event follow-up — ensuring continuity into peer-reviewed publications and long-term collaborations.

Goal 03

Give back to the community

We will coordinate with Rubin's Community Science Team to include the lessons learned in documentation that will benefit the whole Rubin community.

Science Topics

Science topics are proposed by the mentors, and teams are assembled around them in advance. Every team uses at least one LINCC Frameworks tool and at least one community broker. Broad categories, drawn from the projects already proposed, include:

Transients & supernovae

Detecting and characterizing alert-stream transients, including Type Ia supernovae and their host galaxies.

Variable stars & the distance scale

Period-finding and population studies of broker-served variable stars, and distance determination across the nearby Universe.

AGN & alert classification

Characterizing AGN, transients and variable stars in the Rubin alert stream using community brokers such as ALeRCE.

Solar System

Exploring Rubin's Solar System discoveries and connecting them to the Minor Planet Center.

Anomaly detection

Discovering the unexpected — mining the alert stream for rare and anomalous events.

Rubin Data & LINCC Tools

The workshop is hands-on. A short set of plenary talks on Day 1 sets the context — the Rubin ecosystem, a broker crash course, and an Alert Production orientation — and everything else is building. Public datasets beyond Rubin (e.g. Gaia, Roman) broaden the science reach without requiring proprietary access; the LSDB/HATS formats are designed for efficient cross-matching.

  • LSDB / HATS — scalable catalog cross-matching in the HATS format.
  • Hyrax — machine-learning and anomaly-detection workflows on alerts.
  • LightCurveLynx — light-curve modeling and analysis.
  • Community brokers — e.g. ALeRCE; no data rights required to join.
  • Rubin alert packets — the public alert stream, open to every astronomer.
  • Companion surveys — Gaia, ZTF, ATLAS, Roman for cross-matching.

Pre-event (T–2 weeks): a ~2-hour self-paced tutorial covering LSDB/HATS, Hyrax and LightCurveLynx, plus a short kickoff Zoom with each team's mentor to set the science question and divide initial tasks.

Alert brokers

Brokers ingest Rubin's full public alert stream, add value (cross-matches, features, machine-learning classifications), and let you filter and retrieve events for your science. No LSST data rights are required to use them. A great overview and side-by-side comparison lives in the community Broker Wiki:

  • Rubin Broker Wiki ↗ — how to choose a broker for your science case, with a feature comparison across brokers.
  • ALeRCE ↗ — dynamic catalog and broker with cross-matched, annotated, machine-learning-classified light curves.
  • ANTARES ↗ — NOIRLab real-time broker adding context from multiwavelength catalogs and event history.
  • AMPEL ↗ — modular platform for real-time and offline alert processing with explicit provenance tracking.
  • Babamul ↗ — lightweight, open-source broker that restructures the stream into smaller science-ready channels.
  • Fink ↗ — community broker with catalog/stream cross-matches, feature engineering, and machine-learning enrichment.
  • Lasair ↗ — UK broker built around real-time SQL filters, light-curve features, and intelligent cross-match.
  • Pitt-Google ↗ — cloud-based broker and data-distribution service for scalable public access to LSST alerts.

Downstream brokers — such as POI (variable-star light curves) and SNAPS (Solar System) — build on the streams above; see the Broker Wiki for the full list of nine.

LINCC Frameworks tools

LINCC Frameworks (an LSST Discovery Alliance program) builds open-source tools for analysis at LSST scale. Below are the tools we will use, each with documentation, tutorials, and its LSST-DA Slack channel. See the full areas of focus ↗ and the overview tech talk ↗.

LSDB / HATS

Scalable spatial analysis. HATS (Hierarchical Adaptive Tiling System) is a Parquet-based catalog format with dynamic spatial partitioning; LSDB is a Dask-based, pandas-like framework for parallel, distributed analysis and cross-matching over large catalogs. A HATS version of Rubin DP2 is available at data.lsdb.io.

LSDB docs ↗ HATS docs ↗ Rubin tutorials ↗ Datasets ↗ Slack: #lincc-frameworks-lsdb

Hyrax

A low-code framework for training and analyzing machine-learning models reproducibly — cutout acquisition, data loading, hyperparameter tuning, model logging, inference, similarity search, and anomaly detection, scaling from laptop to cloud.

Docs ↗ GitHub ↗ Intro talk (video) ↗ Slack: #lincc-frameworks-hyrax

LightCurveLynx

Flexible, scalable time-domain simulation software with realistic effects and survey strategies, wrapping many existing simulation packages — e.g. to model an expected population (such as Type Ia supernovae) and study selection biases against Rubin data.

Docs ↗ Tutorials ↗ Rubin DP2 tutorial ↗ GitHub ↗ Intro talk (video) ↗ Slack: #lincc-frameworks-lightcurvelynx

RAIL (photo-z, with DESC)

Redshift Assessment Infrastructure Layers — a modular toolkit for Rubin-wide photometric-redshift pipelines: creation of mock photometry, estimation by many algorithms under a shared API, and evaluation of performance metrics.

Docs ↗ GitHub ↗ Slack: #desc-pz-rail

Also in the LINCC Frameworks ecosystem: nested-pandas (scalable time-series tables), KBMOD (moving-object / Solar System detection), and the Python Project Template ↗ for reproducible packages. General help on Slack: #lincc-frameworks-qa · #lincc-tech-talks · #lincc-frameworks-dev

Glossary of key terms

A quick reference to terms you will hear during the workshop. For the complete, official list, see the Rubin/LSST Glossary & Acronyms ↗ (searchable, English & Spanish) and the simpler education glossary ↗.

Vera C. Rubin Observatory
The NSF–DOE observatory in Chile conducting the LSST — an 8.4 m telescope with a 3.2-gigapixel camera imaging the entire southern sky every few nights.
LSST (Legacy Survey of Space and Time)
Rubin's flagship 10-year survey, repeatedly imaging the sky to build a deep, time-resolved map of the Universe.
LSST-DA (LSST Discovery Alliance)
The organization that fosters the LSST science community — hosting the Science Collaborations, LINCC Frameworks, and the community Slack.
LINCC Frameworks
An LSST-DA program developing open-source software (LSDB/HATS, Hyrax, LightCurveLynx, RAIL, KBMOD, …) for astronomy at LSST scale.
Data rights
The formal authorization to access Rubin's proprietary (non-public) data products during the survey. Granted to scientists in the US and Chile and to institutions with in-kind contributions.
Without data rights
Not holding proprietary access. This workshop is designed for exactly this case: the public alert stream and broker products are open to everyone, no data rights required.
Public data
Rubin data products available to anyone, worldwide — above all the alert stream and the broker classifications built on it.
Data products
The catalogs, images, and alerts Rubin generates: Prompt products (nightly, including alerts) and Data Release products (annual, mostly rights-restricted).
Prompt products
Products generated within ~60 seconds of each observation, including difference images and the alerts that flag changes in the sky.
Alert
A message issued when Rubin detects a change (a new, brightening, fading, or moving source) in a difference image. It carries the measurement plus a short history.
Alert packet
The structured data record of a single alert — photometry, position, recent light-curve history, and image cutouts.
Alert stream
The continuous flow of alert packets (up to ~10 million per night) that Rubin broadcasts publicly to the brokers.
Broker
Software that ingests the full alert stream, enriches it (cross-matching, features, machine-learning classification), and lets scientists filter and retrieve events — no data rights required.
Science Collaboration (SC)
A community of scientists organized around an LSST science theme (e.g. AGN, TVS). Several accept members without data rights.
DP1 / DP2 (Data Previews)
Early Rubin data releases used to prepare the community for full operations. A HATS version of DP2 is available for LSDB.
Light curve
A source's brightness measured over time — the core of time-domain astronomy and of many alert-based analyses.
Transient
A short-lived astronomical event that appears and fades — e.g. supernovae, tidal disruption events, kilonovae.
Variable star
A star whose brightness changes over time; period-finding on their light curves supports distance and population studies.
AGN (Active Galactic Nucleus)
The luminous, accreting supermassive black hole at a galaxy's center; often variable, and identifiable in the alert stream.
Cross-match
Associating sources between catalogs by sky position (e.g. matching Rubin alerts to Gaia, ZTF, or ATLAS). LSDB/HATS make this efficient at scale.
HATS / LSDB
HATS is a spatially partitioned catalog format; LSDB is the framework that analyzes and cross-matches HATS catalogs in parallel.
Photometric redshift (photo-z)
An estimate of a galaxy's redshift (distance) from its brightness in several filters rather than a spectrum. Handled by RAIL.
Minor Planet Center (MPC)
The international clearing house for Solar System small-body observations and orbits, to which Rubin's discoveries are reported.

All flights arrive at José María Córdova International Airport (MDE), a small airport located about one hour from Medellín. There are two ways to reach the city:

If you arrive after 8:00 pm, we recommend taking a taxi or Uber from the bus drop-off point (San Diego or Exposiciones) to your hotel.

Code of Conduct

All participants are expected to follow the LINCC Frameworks Code of Conduct and the LSST-DA Code of Conduct throughout all workshop activities, online and offline.

  • We pledge to treat all people with respect and provide a harassment- and bullying-free environment, regardless of personal attributes.
  • We pledge to respect the work of others by recognizing acknowledgment and citation requests of original authors.
  • We pledge to welcome questions and answer them respectfully, paying particular attention to those new to the community.
  • We pledge to be conscientious of the perceptions of the wider community and to respond to criticism respectfully.
  • We pledge to help the entire community follow the code of conduct and not remain silent when we see violations.
